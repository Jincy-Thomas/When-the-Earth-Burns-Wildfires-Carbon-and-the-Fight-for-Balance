# When the Earth Burns: Wildfires, Carbon, and the Fight for Balance

## Project Overview
Global wildfire patterns from 2012 to 2024 were analyzed, focusing on land burned, carbon emissions, and ecosystem impacts. Countries with the largest absolute burned areas and the highest share of land affected were examined. The study assessed how different ecosystems contribute to fire trends and implications for Earth’s carbon balance.

---

## Data Source

Data on wildfires and CO₂ emissions was used:

1. **Annual Area Burnt by Wildfires (2002–2024)** – Global Wildfire Information System (2025)  
   Link: [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org)  

2. **Wildfire Area Burned by Land Cover Type (2002–2022)** – Global Wildfire Information System (2022)  
   Link: [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org)  

3. **Annual CO₂ Emissions from Wildfires (2002–2024)** – Global Wildfire Information System (2025)  
   Link: [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org)  

These datasets provide annual, country-level wildfire area and associated carbon emissions, broken down by land cover type, enabling trend analysis and cross-country comparisons.

## Methodology / What Was Done

- Global wildfire datasets were collected and cleaned  
- Annual burned area and CO₂ emissions were analyzed by country and land cover type  
- Absolute and proportional trends were calculated to highlight regions most affected  
- Ecosystem contributions to wildfire patterns were assessed  
- Static charts and interactive maps were created to visualize trends and carbon impacts  
- Key insights on climate risk and carbon balance were generated

---

## Key Insights

- Countries with the largest absolute burned areas include the Democratic Republic of Congo, Brazil, and Angola  
- Countries with the highest share of land affected include Laos and Cambodia  
- Savannas and tropical forests contribute differently to global wildfire trends  
- Wildfires remain a natural cycle but pose growing climate and carbon risks  
- Visualization of trends enables better understanding of regional and ecological patterns
